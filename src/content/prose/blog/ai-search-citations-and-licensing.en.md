Imagine that someone posts a forum answer explaining how to repair an old device. Later, an AI search result references the answer, one reader follows a link to the forum, and another customer uses content under a contract with the platform. The same answer can appear as a source, draw readers back to the forum, and fall within a content license. Search results, site visits, and contracts each record a different event.

[Episode 008 discusses search results summarizing content](/weekly/008/transcript#quote-28a19414c9555047d24f) and [whether readers continue on to the original site](/weekly/008/transcript#quote-aaf199cae2be7a884934). Understanding where value goes requires looking separately at the evidence left by search presentation, visits to the original site, and platform contracts.

## What does a citation give each party?

In this hypothetical case, the search service can use the forum answer to help its user, who may find a repair method sooner. If the result displays the source, the platform and author may also gain visibility. A citation alone, however, does not prove that anyone opened the original post or that its author earned money.

The practical value of that visibility depends partly on whether the author is named clearly, whether the link is easy to find, and whether the citation preserves context. A reader who does not click may still have found the answer useful. Equally, displaying a source is not itself a recorded visit.

## Clicks and contracts have different records

A click brings a reader to the forum. A follow-up question or a correction can also help the author improve the original answer. The actual benefit depends on how the site records visits, presents authors, and distributes any revenue. It cannot be inferred from the presence of a link.

Google Search Central says that performance from AI Overviews and AI Mode is included in Search Console’s overall web search performance reporting. That aggregate can help a site observe search performance, but it cannot by itself reveal how many clicks came from one particular AI citation. Nor does it establish that traffic necessarily flows toward or away from a particular site.

Licensing follows a different record. Reddit’s 2025 Form 10-K describes contracts that grant customers access to platform content, generally for fixed fees or usage-based charges, with revenue recognized as partners benefit from use over the contract term. Its other revenue category also includes products sold directly to users, so the entire category cannot be treated as licensing revenue. Contract revenue is a different record from a click on an individual answer. The filing does not assign revenue to each citation.

## The author’s share needs a separate answer

A platform’s licensing contracts do not, on the basis of the filing alone, establish whether or how each author is paid, or that the platform has identical contractual rights to all content. An author may receive readership and community feedback, and may receive other benefits under a particular arrangement. Establishing the latter requires checking the applicable terms or agreement.

A site operator can investigate three questions separately: whether a source appears in search, how many identifiable visits the site receives, and what content a license covers and how it is priced. Authors need a separate inquiry into attribution, visibility, and compensation. Comparing those records side by side helps avoid counting a citation as a click or allocating a contract’s revenue directly to one answer.
