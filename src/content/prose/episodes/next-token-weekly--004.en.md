Models are taking on editing work. Agents are helping with everyday tasks. What does that leave for product managers?

On the Mid-Autumn Festival, our four co-hosts return to Xiangwai Space to discuss Opus 5.5 and Muse.

We start with promotional videos, pixel animation and drawing demonstrations. When a model considers texture, rhythm, sound and transitions together, how does that change creative work? Can a carefully written skill hold a newer model back?

Muse brings the discussion to everyday product design: connectors that appear when needed, tasks that continue during a conversation, and to-do lists that can be remembered and checked off. Why should someone seeking a helpful assistant first have to learn about keys, MCP, skills and sessions?

The second half turns to building a business and a body of work: when to reduce model costs, where Jev fits, and why distribution, service and trust still matter when creating software becomes easier.

Recorded at Xiangwai Space on September 25, 2026. The episode includes personal experiences, opinions and predictions, rather than independent verification of product capabilities.

## Questions from the episode

- How far can models reach into creative work?
- When does prescribing steps get in the way of communicating intent?
- What makes an agent useful beyond a long feature list?
- Why do tasks, files and reviewable results matter alongside chat?
- Should a startup find product-market fit before optimizing model costs?
- What earns attention and trust when everyone can build a product?
