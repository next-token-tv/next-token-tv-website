## Arm

Arm (Arm Holdings plc, often rendered 安谋 in Chinese) is a UK company that designs processor [architecture](/en/wiki/terms/cpu) and semiconductor IP, headquartered in Cambridge. It does not fabricate chips itself; instead, it licenses processor designs to chip companies, which integrate them into their own products and have them manufactured by foundries — so "Arm" refers both to the company and, colloquially, to the ARM architecture it licenses. The acronym ARM first appeared in 1983 as Acorn RISC Machine and later stood for Advanced RISC Machines.

## Business and licensing model

Arm's revenue comes from IP licensing: customers license processor designs or the architecture itself and pay license fees and royalties as products ship. The company describes this as a "success-based business model" and offers programs such as Arm Flexible Access (low-cost access to IP, with license fees due only at tape-out) and Arm Total Access (a more comprehensive package of IP, tools, and support).

Its product lines are layered by use case: Cortex-A (application CPUs), Cortex-R (real-time CPUs), Cortex-M (microcontrollers), Cortex-X (flagship performance cores), Neoverse (servers and infrastructure), Mali GPUs and Ethos NPUs, plus Compute Subsystems (CSS) that package CPUs, interconnect, and system IP for delivery. The [official company page](https://www.arm.com/company) states that more than 350 billion Arm-based chips have shipped to date.

## Company history

The company was founded in Cambridge on November 12, 1990, spun out of Acorn Computers and structured as a joint venture between Acorn, Apple, and VLSI Technology, with Apple providing early investment; Robin Saxby served as the first CEO. SoftBank Group acquired Arm in 2016; a planned acquisition by NVIDIA announced in 2020 was terminated in February 2022, and Arm listed on the Nasdaq in 2023. Current business and product information is on the [official company page](https://www.arm.com/company).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Arm a company or an architecture?

Both. Arm Holdings plc is the UK company that designs and licenses processor architecture and IP; "the ARM architecture" refers to the instruction set and processor designs it licenses. ARM originally stood for Acorn RISC Machine; the company was incorporated in November 1990 (initially as Widelogic Limited) and renamed Advanced RISC Machines Limited that December.

### What does ARM stand for?

ARM first stood for Acorn RISC Machine (Acorn's RISC processor) and later for Advanced RISC Machines after the company was founded. The company brand is now written "Arm" and is no longer treated as an acronym.

### How does Arm differ from x86?

Arm and x86 are parallel processor architecture families: Arm is licensed to many chip designers and dominates mobile devices while expanding into laptops and servers; x86 is used mainly in PCs and servers by Intel and AMD. For more background on the comparison, see the [x86 entry](/en/wiki/terms/x86).

### Does Arm manufacture chips?

No. Arm's core business is licensing processor designs and architectures; licensed chip companies design the chips and foundries manufacture them, and Arm runs no chip fabs. The official company page also announces that its compute platform is expanding into self-designed silicon products (such as the server-focused Arm AGI CPU); product status is on the [official page](https://www.arm.com/company).

### Who acquired Arm?

SoftBank Group acquired Arm in 2016; after NVIDIA's planned acquisition, announced in 2020, was terminated in February 2022, Arm went public on the Nasdaq in 2023.
