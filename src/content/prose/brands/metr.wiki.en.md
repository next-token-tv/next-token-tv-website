## METR

METR (Model Evaluation and Threat Research) is an independent nonprofit research institute based in Berkeley, California, that evaluates the capabilities of frontier AI models on long-horizon tasks and the risks they may pose (per [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR) and the [official introduction](https://metr.org/about)). It was founded by Beth Barnes in 2022; its predecessor was ARC Evals, the evaluation division of the Alignment Research Center, which spun off as an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit and was renamed METR in December 2023 (per English Wikipedia).

## Research

- Capability evaluations: measuring what AI agents can do on a computer, from small code edits to fully autonomous AI research; representative benchmarks include RE-Bench, which tests whether AI systems can solve research engineering tasks (per the [official introduction](https://metr.org/about) and English Wikipedia).
- Risk evaluations: researching whether agents might take actions no human intended, and whether such actions could be detected and contained; the organization's earlier prototyped approach for scaling safeguards to measured capabilities has since been adopted in policies by multiple AI developers (per the official introduction).
- Work with AI companies: it has partnered with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta and Amazon to pilot frontier risk assessments (per the official introduction).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is METR?

METR (Model Evaluation and Threat Research) is a nonprofit research institute that evaluates the capabilities and risks of frontier AI systems. It was founded by Beth Barnes in 2022 and is based in Berkeley, California; see the [official page](https://metr.org/about).

### What does METR research?

It mainly measures AI agents' ability to complete long-horizon tasks (such as the RE-Bench benchmark and research automation), and evaluates the risks of agent behavior and the effectiveness of mitigations.

### How is METR related to AI companies?

METR has partnered with companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta and Amazon to pilot frontier risk assessments; per its official introduction the organization commits to independent assessment and publishes a conflict-of-interest policy.
