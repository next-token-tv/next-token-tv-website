## TED

TED is a nonprofit organization sharing ideas through talks, conferences, and educational content. Richard Saul Wurman and Harry Marks held the first conference in February 1984, named after its three founding topics — Technology, Entertainment, Design; the conference became annual in 1990. Since 2001, TED has been run by Chris Anderson through a nonprofit foundation (the Sapling Foundation, the TED Foundation since 2019). The official mission statement is to "discover and spread ideas that spark conversation, deepen understanding, and drive meaningful change."

## Events and content

TED's main offerings include the annual TED conference (held in Vancouver, Canada, since 2014); TED Talks videos — free online since June 2006 under Creative Commons licenses; TEDx — independently organized local events that follow TED's event rules; plus TED-Ed educational content, TED podcasts, and TED Courses. Entry points are on [ted.com](https://www.ted.com/) and the [official About page](https://www.ted.com/about).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does TED stand for?

TED comes from Technology, Entertainment, Design — the themes of the first 1984 conference. The content has since broadened to science, culture, the humanities, and more.

### Is TED a nonprofit?

Yes. TED is run through a nonprofit foundation (the Sapling Foundation, the TED Foundation since 2019), supported by members, donors, and partners; see [About TED](https://www.ted.com/about).

### Where can I watch TED Talks for free?

TED Talks have been free online since June 2006; they can be watched on [ted.com](https://www.ted.com/) and official channels such as YouTube, most with subtitles and transcripts.

### How do TEDx events relate to TED?

TEDx events are locally organized events licensed from TED; they follow TED's rules and are separate from the main conference. Organizer entry points are on the [TEDx page](https://www.ted.com/tedx).
