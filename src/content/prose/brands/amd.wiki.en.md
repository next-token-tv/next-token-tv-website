## AMD

AMD (Advanced Micro Devices) is an American semiconductor company that designs CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs, and adaptive SoCs. It was founded on May 1, 1969 by Jerry Sanders and seven colleagues from Fairchild Semiconductor and is headquartered in Santa Clara, California (per [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD)). Lisa Su has served as CEO since October 2014.

## Business and product lines

AMD’s products are organized into several main brands:

- Ryzen and Threadripper: desktop and workstation CPUs.
- EPYC: server and data-center CPUs.
- Radeon: graphics cards and graphics products.
- AMD Instinct: accelerators for AI and high-performance computing.
- Embedded and adaptive SoCs, including the FPGA lines from Xilinx, acquired in 2022.

Drivers and software downloads are available on the [official support page](https://www.amd.com/en/support.html). Historical milestones: AMD acquired graphics company ATI in 2006, spun off manufacturing into GlobalFoundries in 2009, and launched the Zen architecture and Ryzen brand in 2017 (per English Wikipedia).

## Frequently asked questions

### What company is AMD?

AMD is an American semiconductor company that designs CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs, and accelerated-computing products. It was founded in 1969 and is headquartered in Santa Clara, California. See the [official corporate page](https://www.amd.com/en/corporate.html).

### Where is AMD’s official website?

The official website is [amd.com](https://www.amd.com/), with products, support, and driver downloads ([driver download entry](https://www.amd.com/en/support.html)).

### What are Ryzen, EPYC, Radeon, and Instinct?

They are AMD’s product brands by use case: Ryzen for desktop and workstation CPUs, EPYC for server CPUs, Radeon for graphics cards, and Instinct for AI and high-performance-computing accelerators.

### How are AMD and Intel related?

Both are x86 processor makers and longtime competitors in CPUs. AMD became a licensed second source for x86 chips through a 1982 technology-exchange agreement with Intel and shipped its own compatible designs from the 1990s (per English Wikipedia). For current comparisons, use the two companies’ official specification pages.
