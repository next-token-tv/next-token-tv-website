## Epic Games

Epic Games is an American video game and software development and publishing company founded in 1991 by Tim Sweeney ([entry](/en/wiki/people/tim-sweeney)) as Potomac Computer Systems in Maryland, renamed Epic MegaGames in 1992, and given its current name after the headquarters moved to Cary, North Carolina in 1999 (per [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games)).

## Business and products

- [Unreal Engine](/en/wiki/products/unreal-engine): the real-time 3D creation and game development engine developed by the company, also used in its own games.
- Fortnite: the internally developed game released in 2017.
- Epic Games Store: the PC game store launched in 2018.

On ownership, per English Wikipedia Tencent acquired in 2012 a stake equal to roughly 40 percent of total Epic Games equity, while Tim Sweeney remains the controlling shareholder and CEO. The company overview is on the [official page](https://www.epicgames.com/site/about).

## Discussion in the show

Episode 009 touches on Epic Games twice. In the chapter "创始人、组织与消费" ([Founders, organizations and consumption](/weekly/009/transcript#chapter-05)) in the Chinese transcript, Guizang relays Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney's posts pushing "AI-First" game development on X (Twitter): having developers use AI for more content, rewriting the launcher himself with AI, and — in Guizang's view — cutting staff who do not use it, which drew online arguments (see [the passage](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-db88b9b4fb6b7bb1bbe5)). Guizang also relays that Unreal Engine 5.6 is to become "AI-First," aiming to make the engine easier for AI to use, and to re-optimize its adaptation to WebGL and [Three.js](/en/wiki/products/threejs), returning to web distribution (see [the passage](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-3f77d98fdd8d2ceecb83)). In the chapter "软件和游戏网文化与实践" ([Software and games: web-fiction culture and practice](/weekly/009/transcript#chapter-04)), Orange mentions that "the new Unreal or whatever engine" is being optimized for the web (see [the passage](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-36ca95d72bfc25ea5c53)), and Guizang, discussing [Phantom Blade Zero](/en/wiki/products/phantom-blade-zero), relays overseas players' comments that this Unreal Engine 5 game runs smoothly even on low-end hardware without ray tracing (see [the passage](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-a2256f66f19510c953bc)). These are the participants' retellings of public statements and player commentary, not official statements from Epic Games.

## Frequently asked questions

### What company is Epic Games?

An American game company founded by Tim Sweeney in 1991 that develops Fortnite, runs the Epic Games Store, and develops Unreal Engine. See the [official company page](https://www.epicgames.com/site/about).

### Who develops Unreal Engine?

Unreal Engine is developed by Epic Games as a real-time 3D creation and game development engine; see the [Unreal Engine entry](/en/wiki/products/unreal-engine).

### How are Epic Games and Tencent related?

Per English Wikipedia, Tencent acquired in 2012 a stake equal to roughly 40 percent of Epic Games' total equity; Tim Sweeney remains the controlling shareholder and serves as CEO.
