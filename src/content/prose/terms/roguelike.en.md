## Definition

Roguelike is a video game genre descended from the 1980 game Rogue, which was developed by students at the University of California, Berkeley—the genre takes its name from that game.

## How it works

Commonly cited criteria for the "like Rogue" label include turn-based play, grid-based maps, procedurally generated levels and permadeath—when the character dies, progress is wiped and a new run begins from scratch.

## Usage

Classics such as NetHack carried the tradition forward, and popular titles like Diablo drew inspiration from it; lighter variants that soften permadeath and other elements are known as roguelites.

## Related and confusable terms

A [mod](/en/wiki/terms/mod) is a player-made modification of an existing game, while roguelike is a game genre; one is about changing a game, the other about a category of games—they are not on the same dimension.
