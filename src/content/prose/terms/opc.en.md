## Definition

A one-person company (OPC) is a company, or way of working, founded and run by a single person, usually with no or very few employees: product, development, growth, and support are covered by one person with the help of tools, automation, and outsourcing. In the international tech scene it goes hand in hand with independent developers and solo founders.

## How it works

The point of a one-person company is not that one person labors alone, but that automatable parts are delegated to tools: templated products, automated distribution, usage-based infrastructure. AI and agents make "one person doing what used to take a team" realistic, which is why the show returns to the idea repeatedly.

## Usage

This usage appears in Next Token's discussions: in Weekly #001 on AI-native organizations, Guizang said "everyone is full-stack, everyone is an OPC"; Weekly #004 mentioned an independent developer famous for running one, with the reminder that as an individual you should not be trying to go big. In the show's context OPC is a way of working, not a legal form.

## Related and confusable terms

It mirrors [AI-native](/en/wiki/terms/ai-native): AI-native describes how an organization is built, a one-person company describes its size, and in the AI era the two often hold at once. Note that the One Person Company in India's Companies Act, 2013 is a statutory company form (a single shareholder), which differs from the tech-scene usage here.
