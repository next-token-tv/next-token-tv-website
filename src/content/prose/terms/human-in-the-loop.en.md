## Definition

Human in the Loop (HITL) is a mechanism that keeps a person involved in an automated or AI workflow: the machine executes, while a human reviews, decides or corrects at key checkpoints.

## How it works

Typically a model or program produces an output first, then a person confirms, edits or rejects it, and the confirmed result flows back into the process. In model training, humans scoring or ranking outputs are also the core of methods such as [RLHF](/en/wiki/terms/rlhf).

## Usage

HITL appears in workflows with high stakes or accuracy requirements: content moderation, medical image reading, fallback decision-making in autonomous driving, and human approval before an [agent](/en/wiki/terms/agent) takes critical actions.

## Related and confusable terms

HITL stresses human intervention and is the counterpart of a fully autonomous [agent](/en/wiki/terms/agent): an [agent](/en/wiki/terms/agent) completes tasks on its own toward a goal, while a HITL process waits for human sign-off at checkpoints. They are often combined—an agent handles most steps by itself, and key decisions are left to a person.
