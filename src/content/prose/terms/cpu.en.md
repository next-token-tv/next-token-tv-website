## Definition

A central processing unit (CPU) is the chip that executes a program's instructions, performing the arithmetic, logic, and control a system runs on. It is often called the computer's "brain," though strictly speaking it only follows instructions — the loaded program decides what to do.

## How it works

A CPU runs a fetch-decode-execute loop, relying on registers and layered caches to keep the pipeline supplied with data. Modern designs add multiple cores, superscalar issue, out-of-order execution, and branch prediction to extract more parallelism from both single and multiple threads.

## Usage

Nearly every computing device — phones, laptops, servers — uses a CPU as its main processor. Workloads with complex branching and strong serial dependencies, such as compiling code or running databases and business logic, lean heavily on CPU performance.

## Related and confusable terms

A [GPU](/en/wiki/terms/gpu) favors massive parallel throughput, while a CPU favors low-latency execution of intricate logic; a [TPU](/en/wiki/terms/tpu) is a tensor-focused ASIC. At the instruction-set level, [x86](/en/wiki/terms/x86) is one of the most widespread CPU architecture families.
