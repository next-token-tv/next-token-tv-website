## Definition

Artificial superintelligence (ASI) refers to a hypothetical AI system whose intelligence comprehensively and substantially exceeds that of the smartest humans, outperforming them in virtually every domain. "Super" points past matching humans to clearly surpassing them.

## How it works

There is no accepted path to ASI. It is usually discussed as a hypothetical stage after AGI: a system able to improve itself might grow beyond human understanding and control. Such arguments rest on assumptions about how intelligence scales and remain thought experiments.

## Usage

ASI comes up mainly in discussions of long-term AI risk, safety research, and alignment research, where it names an extreme scenario to guard against — not any existing technology.

## Related and confusable terms

The difference between ASI and [artificial general intelligence](/en/wiki/terms/agi) is the capability ceiling: AGI is benchmarked against human level, ASI assumes beyond it. Both are hypothetical stages, distinct from [artificial intelligence](/en/wiki/terms/ai) as a description of real-world technology.
