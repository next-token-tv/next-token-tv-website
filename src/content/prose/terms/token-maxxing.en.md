## Definition

Token Maxxing is a phrase used by the show's participants, not a dictionary term: it means pushing AI usage to the max and consuming tokens at massive scale — letting AI take part in as much of the work as possible. The word form borrows the internet-slang "-maxxing" suffix for maximizing something.

## How it works

It is typically attached to scenes like "everyone building with AI," "frantic iteration," and "mass model calls": individuals handing as much daily work to models as possible, teams converting as many process steps to AI as they can.

## Usage and context

This usage appears in Next Token's discussions: in Weekly #001's segment on AI-native organizations, Yang Pan said "Token Maxxing doesn't work" — big companies had done all the efficiency moves yet still could not find the organizational path; Orange added, "Claude Code is Token Maxxing: they build everything with AI, but the speed didn't come up." In the show's context the term both describes an all-in AI usage pattern and carries the critique that piling up tokens alone does not yield organizational gains; the participants used the word while debating the limits of those gains.

## Related and confusable terms

It often comes up next to the [Jevons paradox](/en/wiki/terms/jevons-paradox): total token consumption rising as efficiency improves and costs fall is the macro observation, while Token Maxxing is the user's deliberate behavior of maxing out usage. It is likewise unrelated to the show's brand, [Next Token](/en/wiki/brands/next-token) — the shared word "token" does not imply any connection.
