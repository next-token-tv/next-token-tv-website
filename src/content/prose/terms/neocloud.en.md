## Definition

Neocloud is a term from the AI era for a new generation of cloud providers focused on GPU capacity for AI training and inference. The word was popularized by the semiconductor industry research firm SemiAnalysis in 2024 reports.

## How it works

A neocloud deploys large numbers of GPU servers in its own or leased [IDC](/en/wiki/terms/idc) facilities and rents them out as bare-metal or virtualized instances, billed by the hour, usually with a thin platform layer for cluster management and scheduling that helps customers scale quickly.

## Usage

Typical customers are startups training large models, inference workloads that need burst capacity, and research teams unwilling to wait in hyperscale cloud quota queues; hourly GPU pricing is the core competitive metric for these providers.

## Related and confusable terms

A neocloud is still a form of [cloud computing](/en/wiki/terms/cloud-computing), but it does not chase the everything-store service catalog of AWS — it focuses on GPUs. The physical facilities it depends on are [IDCs](/en/wiki/terms/idc), and the GPU hardware stack behind it belongs to [infrastructure](/en/wiki/terms/infrastructure) and [GPUs](/en/wiki/terms/gpu).
