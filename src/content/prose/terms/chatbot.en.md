## Definition

A chatbot is a program that interacts with people through a conversational interface, mainly in the form of text Q&A, evolving from early rule-based scripts to large-language-model-driven systems. Its defining trait is conversational input and output rather than graphical interface operation.

## How it works

The typical flow is receiving a user message, generating a reply, and optionally combining retrieval or tools. Chatbots of the rules era relied on fixed scripts and state machines; chatbots of the LLM era have the model generate replies directly, with capability bounded by the model and the context it is given.

## Usage

This usage appears in Next Token's discussions: in Weekly #004, [it was noted in the Chinese transcript that ordinary consumers "open a Chatbot or an Agent and half of them don't know what to do"](/weekly/004/transcript#quote-f84b57fbd69759ac3233), using the chatbot as shorthand for consumer-facing conversational AI products. Customer support, Q&A assistants, and companion apps are its typical settings.

## Related and confusable terms

The common dividing line from an [Agent](/en/wiki/terms/agent): a chatbot's main output is the conversation itself, usually responding within a single turn or session, while an agent aims to complete tasks — planning, calling tools, executing multi-step work in the background. Chatbots today keep absorbing agent capabilities, and at the product level the boundary grows blurrier.
