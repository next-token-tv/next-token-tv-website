## Definition

The GPL (GNU General Public License) is a copyleft license published by the Free Software Foundation: anyone may run, study, modify and redistribute the software, but redistribution must be licensed under the same terms and accompanied by the corresponding source code.

## How it works

The GPL writes "modified and redistributed versions must stay open" directly into its grant, and copyright law enforces this so-called viral clause: once a distributed product includes GPL code, the whole must be offered under the GPL. The versions in common use are GPLv2 and GPLv3, and the Linux kernel is licensed under GPLv2.

## Usage

Prominent software such as the Linux kernel, GCC and WordPress ships under the GPL. Companies using it need to watch the compliance boundary: internal use is generally fine, but distributing a derivative product to third parties triggers the open source obligations.

## Related and confusable terms

[fork](/en/wiki/terms/fork) describes the practice of copying a project and evolving it independently, while the GPL governs the licensing conditions under which a fork may be redistributed; permissive licenses such as MIT and Apache-2.0 do not require derivatives to adopt the same license.
