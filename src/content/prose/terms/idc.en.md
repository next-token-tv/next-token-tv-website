## Definition

An IDC (Internet Data Center) is a purpose-built facility that centrally houses servers, storage and network equipment with reliable power, cooling, bandwidth and operations support — the physical foundation of internet services and cloud computing.

## How it works

Racks are populated according to power budgets, backed by UPS units, precision cooling and fire suppression to keep systems running; customers can rent racks and bandwidth, or rent the [servers](/en/wiki/terms/server) hosted inside.

## Usage

The compute behind cloud providers, video platforms, game companies and financial institutions physically sits in some IDC somewhere; high-power GPU racks in the AI era raise the demands on power and cooling, making IDC capacity a scarce resource in the race for compute.

## Related and confusable terms

An IDC is a physical facility, while [cloud computing](/en/wiki/terms/cloud-computing) is the service model that pools such facilities and sells the resources; [infrastructure](/en/wiki/terms/infrastructure) is the broader idea of the underlying layer, of which an IDC is the physical part.
