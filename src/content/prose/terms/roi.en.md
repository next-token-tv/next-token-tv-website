## Definition

Return on investment (ROI) is a ratio that expresses how much a given outlay earned back, usually computed as net gain divided by cost and quoted as a percentage. It measures the efficiency of an investment rather than its size.

## How it works

ROI is deliberately flexible in scope: it can be applied to a single ad campaign, a piece of equipment, or an entire project or company, which means comparisons across contexts require a consistent definition of gains and costs. The result can be positive or negative — a negative ROI means the outlay lost money.

## Usage

Marketing budget reviews, project approvals and procurement decisions all use ROI to compare the cost-effectiveness of different options. In everyday business talk, "is the ROI worth it" has become shorthand for asking whether something is worth doing.

## Related and confusable terms

ROI measures efficiency while [ARR](/en/wiki/terms/arr) measures scale; the two often appear together but answer different questions. In startup discussions ROI also sits next to [PMF](/en/wiki/terms/pmf) and [VC](/en/wiki/terms/vc), which respectively address demand, funding and returns.
