## Definition

AI native describes a product, company or workflow designed from the ground up with AI as a core capability, rather than bolting AI features onto existing systems afterward. "AI-augmented" treats AI as a supporting tool; AI native puts AI at the core of the system.

## How it works

The mindset shapes every layer: how data is organized, how tasks are handed to models, how interactions are designed around model capabilities, and how the product iterates on model performance. The test is not "does it use AI" but whether the product would still make sense if you removed the AI.

## Usage

Typical examples include agent products whose reasoning engine is a model, companies that have redesigned their business processes around model capabilities, and applications architected from day one as model-plus-tools. The term also describes how a team works — folding AI into its own production process.

## Related and confusable terms

AI native describes how something is built; [AI slop](/en/wiki/terms/ai-slop) describes low-quality output from careless use of generative AI. The two are on different axes — one is an architectural orientation, the other a quality failure. A common building block of AI-native products is the [agent](/en/wiki/terms/agent).
