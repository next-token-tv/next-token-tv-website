## Definition

Universal basic income (UBI) is a policy proposal under which a government or public institution pays every member of a community the same amount of cash on a regular, unconditional basis, with no means test and no work requirement.

## How it works

UBI design revolves around three parameters: the amount, the payout period and the coverage. Supporters argue that unconditional cash provides basic security and simplifies welfare systems; critics focus on fiscal cost and effects on labor supply. Pilots in various places are mostly local, temporary cash transfers and fall short of full UBI.

## Usage

UBI comes up in welfare policy debates and in arguments about how AI automation may affect employment, and it remains a long-running topic in tech and economics circles.

## Related and confusable terms

UBI differs from unemployment benefits and minimum wage: UBI covers everyone unconditionally, while the other two presuppose unemployment or an employment relationship respectively.
