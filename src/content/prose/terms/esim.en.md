## Definition

eSIM (embedded SIM) is a SIM integrated as a chip soldered directly into a device: carrier information is delivered as a profile that is downloaded and activated remotely, with no physical card to insert. Its behavior is defined by GSMA remote SIM provisioning specifications.

## How it works

The device ships with an embedded eUICC chip; after the user picks a plan — often by scanning a QR code — the carrier's encrypted profile is securely downloaded and activated on the chip. One eSIM can hold multiple profiles, so switching carriers no longer means swapping cards.

## Usage

Dual-SIM phones, smartwatches with independent cellular, laptops with mobile broadband, and large IoT deployments all use eSIM to drop the physical card tray. Buying a local data plan online while traveling is an increasingly common use.

## Related and confusable terms

eSIM is not a network generation or a radio technology — it is the user identity module. [5G](/en/wiki/terms/5g) and [Wi-Fi](/en/wiki/terms/wi-fi) describe how a device reaches a network; eSIM describes how a device proves to a cellular network who it is.
