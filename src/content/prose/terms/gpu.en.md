## Definition

A graphics processing unit (GPU) is a processor built for massively parallel computation. It was created to break each on-screen frame into thousands of pixels and vertices computed simultaneously, and now also carries general-purpose workloads such as AI training, inference, and scientific computing.

## How it works

A GPU combines thousands of lightweight cores that execute large batches of similar arithmetic tasks in parallel, trading latency for throughput. Paired with high-bandwidth memory, it excels at matrix math — the shared core of graphics transforms and neural networks.

## Usage

Gaming, 3D rendering, and video encoding are the GPU's traditional territory; deep learning training and inference have made it the center of AI infrastructure. Everyday users touch a GPU indirectly through video decoding in browsers and on-device machine learning features.

## Related and confusable terms

Compared with a [CPU](/en/wiki/terms/cpu), a GPU has many simple cores suited to parallel work rather than branch-heavy logic; a [TPU](/en/wiki/terms/tpu) is a tensor-focused ASIC with a narrower purpose. In the cloud, a [virtual machine](/en/wiki/terms/virtual-machine) can be assigned a vGPU so that several instances share one physical GPU.
