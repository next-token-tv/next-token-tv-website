## Definition

Universal Serial Bus (USB) is a standard for connecting peripherals, defined by the USB Implementers Forum (USB-IF), carrying both data and power between a host and external devices.

## How it works

It uses a host–device architecture: devices attach to the host through standard connectors (such as Type-A and Type-C) and cables, and successive generations of the specification define data rates and power delivery, with host and device negotiating the capabilities they use.

## Usage

USB handles the connection of keyboards, mice, external storage, printers and docking stations, as well as powering and charging phones and laptops.

## Related and confusable terms

USB is a wired interface standard, unlike [5G](/en/wiki/terms/5g) cellular network technology—the former answers how one device attaches to another, while the latter answers how a device joins a wide-area network.
