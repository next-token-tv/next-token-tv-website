## Definition

Cloud computing is the model defined in NIST SP 800-145: ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable resources — networks, servers, storage, applications and services — that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort.

## How it works

A provider pools the compute, storage and networking inside its [IDC](/en/wiki/terms/idc) facilities, and users provision what they need through a console or API, paying by usage. NIST lists five characteristics (on-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, measured service) and three service models: IaaS, PaaS and SaaS.

## Usage

From website hosting and data backup to AI training and inference, the cloud gives teams elastic compute without building their own facilities; capacity scales up at peak and is released afterwards, and cost follows usage.

## Related and confusable terms

Cloud computing is the overall model; a [neocloud](/en/wiki/terms/neocloud) is a newer class of provider within it that focuses on GPU capacity; [infrastructure](/en/wiki/terms/infrastructure) is the underlying layer the cloud abstracts away; a [VPS](/en/wiki/terms/vps) is the earlier, single-virtual-machine form of renting compute.
