## What iMessage is

iMessage is [Apple](/en/wiki/brands/apple)’s end-to-end encrypted messaging service, used to send and receive messages in the Messages app on [iPhone](/en/wiki/products/iphone), iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Apple Vision Pro. According to Apple’s support documentation, when someone sends a message to your email address or phone number using iMessage, all Apple devices set up to receive messages for that address or number receive it, keeping conversations in sync. Some iMessage features require an Apple Account.

## How it differs from SMS and RCS

Points from the official support documentation:

- Channel and cost: iMessage texts go over Wi-Fi or cellular service and do not count against SMS, MMS, or RCS allowances in your cellular messaging plan (cellular data rates may apply).
- Blue and green bubbles: when the other person also uses iMessage, bubbles appear in blue; SMS, MMS, and RCS messages appear in green. A green send button means the message goes through SMS, MMS, or RCS.
- Encryption: iMessage is end-to-end encrypted, and an encrypted conversation shows "Encrypted" at the top; SMS/MMS conversations carry no such protection.
- RCS: when iMessage isn’t available, Messages can send the text as RCS instead; RCS is provided by your carrier, not by Apple.

## Features and boundaries

The official documentation lists features such as text formatting, editing, unsending, scheduling, backgrounds, polls, Memoji, drawings, collaboration, and group conversation management, along with reporting spam to Apple and screening unknown senders. iMessage is available only on Apple devices; conversations with users on Android or other platforms fall back to SMS/RCS or use other messaging apps.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is iMessage different from SMS?

iMessage goes over Wi-Fi or cellular data, does not count against texting allowances, and is end-to-end encrypted between Apple devices with blue bubbles; SMS, MMS, and RCS go through carrier channels and appear in green bubbles. See the [Apple support guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/about-imessage-iph4e9799206/ios).

### Can iMessage be used on Android or Windows?

No. The official documentation lists iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Apple Vision Pro as the supported devices; messages to Android users are sent via SMS or RCS instead.

### Is iMessage secure?

iMessage is end-to-end encrypted, encrypted conversations show "Encrypted" at the top, and Contact Key Verification can further verify contacts; SMS/MMS conversations are not protected in this way while being sent.

### What if iMessage activation fails?

Activation normally requires a network connection, a signed-in Apple Account, and a phone number that can receive text messages; follow the iMessage activation guides on the [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/) site.
