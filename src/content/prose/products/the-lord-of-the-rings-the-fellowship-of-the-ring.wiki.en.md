## The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring

The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring is a 2001 epic fantasy film directed by Peter Jackson, from a screenplay by Fran Walsh, Philippa Boyens and Jackson, adapting the first volume of J. R. R. Tolkien's 1954 novel The Lord of the Rings. It is the first instalment of the film trilogy. The film premiered in London on December 10, 2001, and opened in the United States and other major markets on December 19; at the 74th Academy Awards in 2002 it received 13 nominations and won 4 awards (per [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)). The film page is on [warnerbros.com](https://www.warnerbros.com/movies/lord-rings-fellowship-ring).

## Subject matter and adaptation

Set in Middle-earth, the story follows the Dark Lord Sauron's search for the One Ring, which has come into the hands of the hobbit Frodo Baggins. Frodo and the Fellowship carry the Ring out of the Shire toward Mount Doom to destroy it — the starting point of the trilogy. The sequence in which the flood of the river Bruinen rises in the shape of charging horses against the Nazgûl remains one of the most-cited large-scale visual-effects shots of its era.

## Frequently asked questions

### When was The Fellowship of the Ring released?

It premiered in London on December 10, 2001 and opened in the United States and most major territories on December 19, 2001 (per English Wikipedia).

### Who directed The Fellowship of the Ring?

Peter Jackson, who co-wrote the screenplay with Fran Walsh and Philippa Boyens.

### What is The Fellowship of the Ring adapted from?

Tolkien's 1954 novel The Lord of the Rings, first volume (The Fellowship of the Ring); the film is the first instalment of the three-film adaptation.

### What awards did The Fellowship of the Ring win?

At the 74th Academy Awards in 2002, it received 13 nominations and won 4 awards (per English Wikipedia).

### Which scene is the "water turning into horses" moment from the show?

The visual-effects sequence at the ford of the river Bruinen, where the flood rises in the shape of galloping horses against the Nazgûl; the participants used it to estimate what such a shot cost at the time — their own recollection, not a production figure.
