## What Google Calendar is

Google Calendar is [Google](/en/wiki/brands/google)’s shareable online calendar, described on the official page as an "AI-powered calendar" with the promise to "spend less time planning and more time doing." It is free with a personal Google account and is also a component of Google Workspace; it runs on the web, through Android and iOS apps, and on Wear OS and Apple Watch devices, with data synced across platforms. The feature list lives on the [official Workspace product page](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/).

## Usage and boundaries

Google Calendar is organized around two kinds of objects — calendars and events:

- Sharing and multiple calendars: the official page says "shared calendars keep everyone in sync" and make it easy to see times that work across schedules; users can also keep separate calendars for work and personal events and color-code them.
- Appointment schedules: a booking page can be set up directly in Calendar so others can pick a slot, with no other apps or downloads needed. A personal Google account gets one free booking page, while Gemini integration, unlimited booking pages, and payments through Stripe require Workspace, Google One, or Business/Enterprise plans.
- Gmail integration: when event details are detected in Gmail, an "Add to Calendar" button appears; flights, hotels, and restaurant reservations in your inbox are automatically added.
- Tasks and focus time: tasks given a date and time automatically appear on the calendar; focus time can silence notifications and auto-decline meetings.
- Other features: file attachments on events, working location and hours, one-click [Google Meet](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/) conferencing, Time Insights schedule analysis, and migration from Microsoft and IBM calendars.

The exact free-versus-paid boundary follows the official page; specific plan tiers and prices are listed on the [official Workspace product page](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Calendar free?

Yes. Google Calendar is free to use with a personal Google account, including one free booking page; Gemini integration, unlimited booking pages, and Stripe payments require Workspace, Google One, or Business/Enterprise plans, as the [official product page](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/) explains.

### Is there a desktop version of Google Calendar?

The official entry points are the web app at [calendar.google.com](https://calendar.google.com/) and the Android and iOS apps, with additional support on Wear OS and Apple Watch. The official product page does not list a standalone Windows or macOS desktop client.

### How do I share my Google Calendar?

Google Calendar supports shared calendars with view or edit permissions as needed, for teams and families. The official sharing instructions are in the [Calendar help center](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37095).

### What is Google Calendar's appointment schedule?

Appointment schedules are Calendar's built-in booking feature: you set bookable time slots in your calendar, and others pick a time on your booking page without extra apps. A personal Google account includes one free booking page; unlimited booking pages belong to the paid plans, per the [official product page](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/).

### How does Google Calendar work with Gmail?

Per the official page: when event details are detected in Gmail, an "Add to Calendar" button appears, and flights, hotels, and restaurant reservations in your inbox are automatically added to Calendar. With Gemini, it can also suggest meeting times based on email context and your calendar's availability.
