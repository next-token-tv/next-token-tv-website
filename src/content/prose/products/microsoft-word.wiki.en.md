## Microsoft Word

Microsoft Word is Microsoft's word processor for writing, formatting, and co-editing documents; see the [official overview](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/word). It is a component of the [Microsoft Office](/en/wiki/products/microsoft-office) suite and has long been sold with the suite as well as on its own.

## Origins and history

According to [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Word), Word began as "Multi-Tool Word for Xenix" in 1983, was soon renamed Microsoft Word, and was first released on October 25, 1983. Development was led by Charles Simonyi, who joined Microsoft in 1981 after working on Bravo, the first GUI word processor, at Xerox PARC; Richard Brodie was the primary software engineer. The first version targeted Xenix, followed by versions for DOS (1983), classic Mac OS (1985), and Windows (1989). Free demo disks were bundled with the November 1983 issue of PC World — among the first instances of software distributed on-disk with a magazine.

In the 1990s Word overtook WordPerfect on Windows and became the de facto standard word processor (per English Wikipedia): Dataquest estimated Word held roughly 55 percent of the Windows word processor market in 1991 and about 80 percent of the worldwide market in the first quarter of 1996. Bundling with the [Office suite](/en/wiki/products/microsoft-office) further expanded its installed base.

## Frequently asked questions

### When was Microsoft Word created?

1983. It began as "Multi-Tool Word for Xenix," was soon renamed Microsoft Word, and was first released on October 25, 1983 (per English Wikipedia).

### Who developed Microsoft Word?

Development was led by Charles Simonyi, who joined Microsoft in 1981, with Richard Brodie as the primary software engineer (per English Wikipedia). Simonyi had previously worked on Bravo, the first GUI word processor, at Xerox PARC.

### How does Word relate to Office and Microsoft 365?

Word is the word processing component of the Office suite, alongside Excel, PowerPoint, and other apps, delivered with Office and later Microsoft 365 subscriptions; see the [official overview](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/word).

### Why did Word become the dominant word processor?

Per English Wikipedia, Word overtook WordPerfect as the de facto standard in the 1990s as Windows spread: Dataquest estimated roughly 55 percent of the Windows word processor market in 1991 and about 80 percent of the worldwide market in the first quarter of 1996. Bundling with the Office suite was a major distribution channel.
