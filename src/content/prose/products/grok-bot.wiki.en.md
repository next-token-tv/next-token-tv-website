## What is Grok Bot

Grok Bot is an autonomous agent product in the Grok line from SpaceXAI — the brand that xAI became after SpaceX [announced its acquisition in February 2026](https://x.ai/news/xai-joins-spacex). The official [documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) describes the product as "AI teammates with names, jobs, and context that compounds over time": you assign tasks by messaging, the Bot carries multi-step work across apps and websites in the cloud, and returns when your approval is needed, working autonomously around the clock.

It occupies the "persistent cloud environment" layer: per the [official documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview), each Bot runs on a persistent cloud computer equipped with a browser, filesystem, and terminal, and those computers run in Cursor's cloud. That is a different shape from the self-hosted [OpenClaw](/en/wiki/products/openclaw) (which runs on your own devices), the framework-oriented [DeepSeek Harness](/en/wiki/products/deepseek-harness), and the desktop office product [WorkBuddy](/en/wiki/products/workbuddy).

## Usage and boundaries

Per the official documentation, you work with a Bot by messaging it: give it a task, context, and access to the tools it needs, and it works on its cloud computer using the browser, filesystem, and terminal. All Bots under your account share one computer — files, browser sessions, and logins are shared across your Bots — while isolation between users is strict, and Bots have no access by default beyond accounts you explicitly sign them into. Bots also learn from demonstration: walk one through a process once and it saves the routine as a reusable skill, and Bots can message each other and share context.

Per the official documentation, clients cover macOS, Windows, and Linux on desktop plus iOS and Android on mobile. Access: the official documentation states Grok Bot is included with every paid individual Cursor plan and the Cursor Teams plan, or you can link an individual SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, or SuperGrok Heavy subscription; usage resets weekly, and no free tier is listed in the documentation.

## Discussion in the show

In Weekly #001's chapter [Grok Bot: why agents need a cloud computer that never goes offline](/weekly/001/transcript#chapter-16), Xiangyang Qiaomu mentions that an Android version shipped that very morning after an iOS-only period; Yang Pan [calls it the future form in his view, arguing AI needs to be online 24 hours a day on a persistent cloud that never loses data](/weekly/001/transcript#quote-b730e5f9ca546bc92abb) — his personal judgment. Guizang describes its progressive authorization from experience: [when the Bot needed his Gmail, Google popped up a card and one tap authorized it](/weekly/001/transcript#quote-0a522a497a56b1a8ffe9); Xiangyang Qiaomu also recounts having it test the top ten Product Hunt products from its cloud computer using his Google account.

In Weekly #002's chapter [Grok Bot / Muse: why agents need a cloud computer](/weekly/002/transcript#chapter-13), Yang Pan [summarizes it as being assigned a computer in the cloud](/weekly/002/transcript#quote-af1872f1d5640ea3a393) and [urges everyone to try an agent with a cloud computer at least once](/weekly/002/transcript#quote-403ec584da2686d9ad06); Guizang points out it has no free quota at all, because starting a virtual machine is a cost from the first message. In [Temporary environments versus a persistent computer](/weekly/002/transcript#chapter-24), [Guizang stresses that Grok Bot never shuts down — installed Skills and login states stay inside](/weekly/002/transcript#quote-73c44181529be6c784bc), and Yang Pan notes there was no fully comparable domestic product at the time. All of the above are participant experiences and opinions, not official statements. All chapters linked are in the Chinese transcript; an English transcript is not available.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Grok Bot?

It is an autonomous agent from SpaceXAI (Grok product line): message-driven "AI teammates" that complete multi-step, cross-app work on a persistent cloud computer with a browser, filesystem, and terminal. See the [official announcement](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-for-enterprise) and [documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview).

### How is Grok Bot priced, and is there a free tier?

The official documentation states Grok Bot is included with every paid individual Cursor plan and the Cursor Teams plan, or usable by linking an individual SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, or SuperGrok Heavy subscription, with weekly usage resets; the documentation lists no free tier. On the show, Guizang likewise described it from a user's perspective as having no free quota because every session starts a virtual machine.

### What is the relationship between Grok Bot and Cursor?

The official documentation says the Bots' computers run in Cursor's cloud and that access is included with paid Cursor plans, or can come through a linked SuperGrok subscription. On Weekly #001, Yang Pan described the two as "the same family, but different product lines" when discussing Cursor's hosted agent — a participant's understanding.

### Which platforms does Grok Bot support?

The official documentation lists desktop clients for macOS (Apple silicon and Intel), Windows, and Linux, plus mobile apps for iOS and Android. The Android version had just shipped in the week Weekly #001 was recorded; before that, mobile was iOS only.

### Does the Grok Bot cloud computer keep my data?

The official documentation says each Bot runs on a persistent cloud computer, all your Bots share that computer with context that compounds over time, isolation between users is strict, and Bots have no access by default beyond accounts you explicitly sign into. Guizang's experience on the show was that it "never shuts down" and installed Skills and login states persist. Retention and deletion details are governed by the official documentation.

### What can Grok Bot do?

The official positioning is multi-step work across apps and websites: operating a browser and files on its cloud computer, learning repeatable routines from demonstration, coordinating multiple Bots in parallel, and reporting back for approvals. The concrete examples on the show (site testing, reading mail) come from participants' personal experience, not an official scenario list.
