## What Obsidian is

Obsidian is a note-taking and knowledge management application built on local Markdown files; its maker describes it as "the free and flexible app for your private thoughts." Notes are stored as plain-text Markdown on your own device, and the official site stresses that "no one else can read them, not even us." There is no vendor lock-in, and files remain directly accessible offline.

## Usage and pricing boundaries

The core Obsidian app is free and runs on desktop and mobile. Notes can be linked to each other and visualized in a graph; Canvas offers a whiteboard-style space, and community plugins and themes are the main extension mechanism.

Beyond the core app, the company sells two subscriptions: [Sync](https://obsidian.md/sync) adds end-to-end encrypted multi-device syncing and version history, and [Publish](https://obsidian.md/publish) turns notes into a hosted site with a custom domain. Commercial use itself is free — the license page states the app is "free for all purposes, including personal, commercial, and non-profit use," with optional commercial licenses for organizations that want to support development. Details are on the [official license page](https://obsidian.md/license).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Obsidian?

A note-taking and knowledge management app based on local Markdown files, with linked notes, graphs, Canvas, and community plugins. The core app is free; see [obsidian.md](https://obsidian.md/).

### Is Obsidian free, and does commercial use require payment?

The core app is free without limits; the license page states commercial use is also free, and organizations may optionally buy an annual commercial license to support development. Terms are on the [official license page](https://obsidian.md/license).

### Where are Obsidian notes stored?

As plain-text Markdown files on your own device, without requiring the company's servers; cross-device syncing needs the Sync subscription or another sync solution of your choice.

### What are Obsidian Sync and Publish?

They are the official paid services: Sync provides end-to-end encrypted multi-device syncing and version history, while Publish turns notes into a publicly accessible knowledge-base site. Whether you need them depends on your workflow; see the [official site](https://obsidian.md/).
