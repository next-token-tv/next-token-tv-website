## What Apple Pay is

Apple Pay is the digital payment service from [Apple](/en/wiki/brands/apple), announced on September 9, 2014 alongside the iPhone 6 and launched in the United States on October 20, 2014; in February 2016 it entered mainland China in partnership with UnionPay. It stores your physical cards in the Apple Wallet app, pays in stores through NFC contactless readers, and checks out in apps and on the web through the Apple Pay button.

The core of its security is tokenization: when you add a card, the device generates a device-specific Device Account Number, and each transaction uses a one-time dynamic security code. The original card number is neither stored on the device nor kept by Apple, so merchants receive a token instead of your real card. Payment is confirmed with Face ID, Touch ID, or the device passcode.

## Uses and differences

Officially supported devices include iPhone, Apple Watch, Mac, iPad, and Apple Vision Pro. In stores, look for the Contactless symbol and hold your iPhone nearby; transit systems in some cities support Express Mode, paying without unlocking the device. Web payments work in Safari and, with participating merchants, third-party browsers.

Three names to distinguish: Apple Wallet is the app that holds your cards and passes; Apple Pay is the payment capability itself; Apple Cash is a peer-to-peer balance service for the U.S. market, sitting at a different layer. The list of countries and banks changes regularly — check the [official page](https://www.apple.com/apple-pay/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I use Apple Pay?

Add your bank card in Apple Wallet following your bank's instructions; in stores, hold your iPhone or Apple Watch near a reader showing the Contactless symbol and confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode; in apps and on the web, choose the Apple Pay button at checkout. Setup steps are in the [official Apple guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT204506).

### Which devices does Apple Pay support?

Officially iPhone, Apple Watch, Mac, iPad, and Apple Vision Pro. Usage differs by device: iPhone and Apple Watch handle in-store contactless payments, while Mac and iPad are mainly for web and app checkouts.

### Does Apple Pay cost anything?

Apple's official materials do not list Apple Pay as a fee-charging service for users; annual fees, surcharges, or foreign-transaction fees on the underlying card depend on your bank and card terms, not on whether the payment goes through Apple Pay.

### Is Apple Pay safe? Can my card number leak?

Apple Pay does not store your original card number on the device or on Apple servers: adding a card creates a device-specific token, and each transaction carries a one-time dynamic security code, so merchants never see the real number. That reduces card-number exposure, but no payment method eliminates fraud outright — report unusual transactions to your issuing bank immediately.

### Which countries and banks support Apple Pay?

The list of supported countries, regions, and partner banks is continuously updated on the [official Apple Pay page](https://www.apple.com/apple-pay/). Whether one specific card works is decided by the issuing bank and by what the Wallet app shows when you add it.

### Can fraud still happen with Apple Pay?

Residual risk exists: in episode 008, Guizang relayed Xiaohongshu posts about batch fraud against one bank's Apple Pay users (charges from South America; the bank unnamed), and Orange said he removed his credit cards from Apple Pay. These are unverified personal accounts; the right response to fraud is to freeze the card and contact the issuing bank, not to draw conclusions about a payment tool.
