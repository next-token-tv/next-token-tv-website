## What QQ is

QQ is an instant messaging application from Tencent, supporting text, voice, and video chat, file transfer, and group chat. It launched on February 10, 1999 under the name "OICQ" (OPEN-ICQ); in 2000, after losing a domain arbitration dispute with AOL's ICQ, Tencent enabled the tencent.com domain and renamed the product "腾讯QQ" (Tencent QQ) (per [Chinese Wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%85%BE%E8%AE%AFQQ) and [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent_QQ)). The official entry point is [im.qq.com](https://im.qq.com); QQ belongs to [Tencent](/en/wiki/brands/tencent).

## From messenger to community

- QQ groups: multi-user chat rooms introduced in 2002, with shared files, photo albums, and more (per Chinese Wikipedia).
- QQ Show: a paid service for dressing up the user's avatar, with most items bought using Q coins (per Chinese Wikipedia); English Wikipedia ties it to the Red Diamond membership scheme.
- Client evolution: in 2023 the Windows client moved to the NT-based architecture, which the official notes describe as unifying the desktop experience across three platforms (per Chinese Wikipedia).

On scale, English Wikipedia records roughly 597 million monthly active QQ accounts as of March 2023.

Episode 009, in the chapter "Harness 与插件生态" ([the Harness and plugin ecosystem](/weekly/009/transcript#chapter-01)), discusses skinning and desktop-pet plugins spreading among young users. Orange compares this "spreading outside the mainstream view" to anime subculture, noting that those users "[have their own networks — maybe QQ — and their own language](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-8d67356689b55384ebfd)" in the Chinese transcript. He then uses QQ Show and Qzone as analogies for the value of "skinning" plugins: ["How can you say Qzone is useless? How much economic value did Qzone have?"](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-76ebe5300ef417e2ec30). Yang Pan quips that ["the moment you mention QQ Show, people say that's too old"](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-1fc2248acd728bb91240). These are nostalgic analogies about a model in which young people pay for dress-up and emotional value — they are not descriptions of QQ's current state.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is QQ?

Tencent's instant messaging app, launched in February 1999 and renamed from OICQ, supporting text, voice, and video chat and groups. The official entry is [im.qq.com](https://im.qq.com).

### How are QQ and WeChat related?

Both are Tencent instant messaging products: QQ launched in February 1999 with multi-platform messaging and community features, while WeChat was first released in 2011 with a mobile-first focus (per English Wikipedia).

### What is QQ Show?

QQ's avatar dress-up service, where users buy clothing and accessories for their virtual avatar with Q coins, tied to the Red Diamond membership (per Chinese and English Wikipedia); the show's participants cite it as the model case of paying for dress-up.

### How many people still use QQ?

Per the figure recorded on English Wikipedia, about 597 million monthly active accounts as of March 2023; for current scale, see Tencent's own disclosures.

### What about Qzone?

Tencent's social platform launched in 2005, built on QQ friend relationships; see the [Qzone entry](/en/wiki/products/qzone).
