## Definition

Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind is a nonfiction book by the Israeli historian [Yuval Noah Harari](/en/wiki/people/yuval-noah-harari), based on the lectures he gave at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. It was first published in Hebrew in Israel in 2011, with the English edition following in 2014. The book surveys human history from the Cognitive Revolution and the Agricultural Revolution to the unification of humankind and the Scientific Revolution, arguing that Homo sapiens came to dominate the world through the capacity to cooperate flexibly in large numbers by believing in shared fictions such as gods, nations, money, and companies. It has been translated into dozens of languages and is among the most widely read big-history works of recent decades.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who wrote Sapiens?

The Israeli historian Yuval Noah Harari, who has been a lecturer at the Department of History of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem.

### When was Sapiens published?

The Hebrew original was first published in Israel in 2011; the English edition followed in 2014.

### What is Sapiens about?

The book traces human history from the Cognitive Revolution through the Agricultural Revolution to the Scientific Revolution, arguing that Sapiens' large-scale cooperation rests on shared belief in fictions.
