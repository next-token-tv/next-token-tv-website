## Definition

Kindle is Amazon’s e-reader family, built primarily for reading digital books, first introduced in 2007. Per the [official buyer’s guide](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/which-kindle-to-buy), the current lineup includes the base Kindle, Paperwhite, the color-screen Colorsoft, the pen-enabled Scribe, and Kids editions.

## How it works

Kindles use e-ink displays; the official page cites front lights and battery life measured in weeks (six to eight weeks depending on model). Reading content comes from the Amazon ebook store, and personal documents can be brought in via Send to Kindle — the officially listed types are PDF, DOC, DOCX, TXT, RTF, HTM, HTML, PNG, GIF, JPG, JPEG, BMP, and EPUB. The Scribe models add a pen for handwriting, including annotations on book pages (Active Canvas) and notes.

## Usage

Typical use is reading ebooks and long documents: e-ink suits extended reading sessions, and multi-week battery life suits travel. The Scribe serves readers who want note-taking and handwritten annotation alongside their books.

## Related and confusable terms

Handwriting is limited to the Scribe range and should not be assumed for the standard readers; the Kindle hardware devices and the Kindle mobile app are different form factors. Kindle is a device line owned by [Amazon](/en/wiki/products/amazon); company-level information lives in the Amazon entry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every Kindle support handwriting?

No. Per the official buyer’s guide, handwriting and note-taking are Scribe features; other models are reading-first. See the [official product page](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/which-kindle-to-buy).

### Which document formats does Kindle support?

The official Send to Kindle page lists PDF, DOC, DOCX, TXT, RTF, HTM, HTML, PNG, GIF, JPG, JPEG, BMP, and EPUB, with a 200 MB per-file limit. See the [official note](https://www.amazon.com/sendtokindle).

### Where can I compare Kindle models?

The official buyer’s guide lays out screen, storage, battery, and waterproofing differences per model at the [Amazon Kindle buyer’s guide](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/which-kindle-to-buy).
