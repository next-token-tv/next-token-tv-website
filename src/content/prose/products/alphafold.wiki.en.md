## What AlphaFold is

AlphaFold is a family of biomolecular structure prediction models developed by [Google DeepMind](/en/wiki/brands/google-deepmind), used to predict the three-dimensional structures of proteins and other molecules from their sequences. In December 2018 the first AlphaFold placed first at CASP13; on November 30, 2020, AlphaFold2 won CASP14 by a large margin and was recognized by the CASP organizers as a solution to the 50-year-old protein-folding problem. The methodology was published in Nature with open-sourced code on July 15, 2021; a week later DeepMind and EMBL-EBI launched the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database; on July 28, 2022, the database expanded past 200 million predicted structures, covering nearly all catalogued proteins known to science at the time.

On May 8, 2024, Google DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs released AlphaFold 3, extending prediction from protein structures to interactions between proteins and DNA, RNA, ligands, and other molecules, alongside AlphaFold Server, a free entry point for non-commercial research. On October 9, 2024, the AlphaFold work earned Demis Hassabis and John Jumper a share of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry (the other half went to David Baker for computational protein design). Google DeepMind states that researchers in more than 190 countries use AlphaFold.

## Uses and boundaries

Researchers use three official entry points: the free [AlphaFold Protein Structure Database](https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/) for looking up existing predictions; [AlphaFold Server](https://alphafoldserver.com/about) for predicting interactions of proteins with ligands and nucleic acids online; and, for self-hosting, the AlphaFold 3 model code and weights are downloadable for academic use, with commercial use requiring a separate agreement with Google DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs.

AlphaFold outputs are computational predictions, not experimentally determined structures, and they do not replace wet-lab work or clinical validation — structure is one step among many in drug development. In the show's discussion of AI pharma, participants likewise stressed that clinical stages cannot be skipped with AI.

## Related concepts

AlphaFold is the model family; the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database is the free repository of predictions; AlphaFold Server is the online prediction interface. None of them is a drug company: Isomorphic Labs is the independent company spun out of [Google DeepMind](/en/wiki/brands/google-deepmind) in 2021 to apply AI to drug design — related to AlphaFold, but a separate entity.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AlphaFold free?

The entry points differ: the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database is fully free; AlphaFold Server is free for non-commercial research; the AlphaFold 3 code and weights are downloadable for academic use, and commercial use requires an agreement with Google DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs. Check the [official AlphaFold page](https://deepmind.google/science/alphafold/) for current terms.

### How do AlphaFold 2 and AlphaFold 3 differ?

AlphaFold 2 (winner of CASP14 in November 2020) predicts the 3D structure of proteins on their own; AlphaFold 3 (released May 8, 2024) extends prediction to the structures and interactions of proteins with DNA, RNA, ligands, and other life molecules. The former is open source; the latter is offered through AlphaFold Server and academic downloads.

### Where can I look up AlphaFold-predicted protein structures?

Search the [AlphaFold Protein Structure Database](https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/) by protein name, UniProt ID, or sequence. Since July 2022 it has held over 200 million predicted structures, free to view and download.

### Can AlphaFold be used to develop new drugs?

AlphaFold predicts target structures and interactions, which makes it a valuable tool in drug design, but it cannot replace experimental validation or clinical trials — the clinical stage cannot be skipped with AI. The episode 008 discussion likewise names phase 3 trials as the current bottleneck for AI pharma. Commercial drug development with AlphaFold is carried out by Isomorphic Labs.

### Why did AlphaFold win a Nobel Prize?

On October 9, 2024, half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for protein structure prediction with AlphaFold; the other half went to David Baker for computational protein design. See the [Nobel Prize announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/summary/).
