## What Bitcoin is

Bitcoin (BTC) is an open-source digital currency system that runs on a peer-to-peer network. The official wording on [Bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/) is that Bitcoin “uses peer-to-peer technology to operate with no central authority or banks; managing transactions and the issuing of bitcoins is carried out collectively by the network,” and that “Bitcoin is open-source; its design is public, nobody owns or controls Bitcoin.”

The project’s origin has a clear timeline: on October 31, 2008, a whitepaper titled “Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System,” authored under the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto, was posted to a cryptography mailing list; Nakamoto’s identity remains unknown. On January 3, 2009, the first block of the ledger (the genesis block) was created, the open-source implementation was released, and the network began operating.

## How it works and what participation means

The Bitcoin network is maintained by nodes around the world. Open-source software such as Bitcoin Core connects to the peer-to-peer network, downloads, and fully validates all blocks and transactions; the original implementation is released under the MIT license, and anyone can run a full node to participate in validation. New blocks are produced through proof of work (based on SHA-256), and the node that produces a block receives a block reward. The reward is halved every 210,000 blocks, capping the total issuance at 21 million bitcoins.

For users, bitcoins are controlled by private keys: self-custody means you alone are responsible for the asset, and confirmed transactions are generally irreversible. Bitcoin’s market price is set by trading venues and has historically been volatile; price information belongs on market-data services, not on any single page. This page only covers technical and historical background — it provides no investment advice and makes no price predictions.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is an open-source digital currency system running on a peer-to-peer network (BTC): no central authority or bank, transaction management and issuance handled collectively by the network, using proof of work and a public distributed ledger.

### When was Bitcoin created?

The whitepaper was posted to a cryptography mailing list on October 31, 2008 under the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto. The network started on January 3, 2009 with the genesis block, and the open-source implementation was released around the same time. Nakamoto’s identity remains unknown.

### What is Bitcoin’s total supply?

Under the current issuance schedule, the block reward halves every 210,000 blocks, capping total issuance at 21 million bitcoins; new coins enter circulation only through block rewards.

### Is Bitcoin open source?

Yes. Bitcoin’s reference implementation, Bitcoin Core, is open source under the MIT license, the code is public, and anyone can run a full node to validate every block and transaction.

### What is Bitcoin mining?

Mining is the process by which nodes compete for the right to add blocks through proof of work: repeatedly computing SHA-256 hashes to produce a qualifying new block, with the winner receiving the block reward and transaction fees. This mechanism both issues new coins and confirms transactions; see [how Bitcoin works on Bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/en/how-it-works).

### What is Bitcoin’s price today, and should I buy it?

Real-time prices belong to market-data services; this page offers no investment advice. Bitcoin has no official price; it is set by trading venues and has historically been volatile. Make independent decisions after understanding the risks of key custody and irreversible transactions.
