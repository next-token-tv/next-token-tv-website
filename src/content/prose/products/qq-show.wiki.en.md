## QQ Show

QQ Show is a virtual-avatar dress-up service that [Tencent](/en/wiki/brands/tencent) launched in 2003 alongside its instant messenger [QQ](/en/wiki/products/qq). Users buy virtual clothing, scenes, and looks with Q coins in the QQ Show mall to decorate their avatar; hovering over a contact's avatar in QQ reveals their QQ Show, and the avatar also appears in QQ community, dating, and chat-room spaces (per [a Sina Tech introduction piece dated February 19, 2003](https://tech.sina.com.cn/it2/2003-02-19/1108166450.shtml), which noted the service was in a free test period until March 1 that year).

## QQ Show in Chinese internet culture

QQ Show is often cited as an early Chinese example of paying for a virtual self: the money bought no physical goods, only personal expression next to a chat avatar. The dress-up features that grew around QQ (QQ Show, QQ Zone decoration and the like) are frequently compared with game skins, theme reskins, and avatar accessories. In Chinese internet slang, "QQ Show" also works as a nostalgia marker for the generation that grew up online in the early 2000s.

## Discussion in the show

Episode 009, chapter 1 ("Harness 与插件生态" — [Harness and the plugin ecosystem](/weekly/009/transcript#chapter-01)), discusses the skin-changing and desktop-pet plugins popular with AI coding tools. Orange likens these "useless" plugins to QQ Show: they amount to reskinning software, and dress-up features carry real value for young users — ["this is QQ Show, how can you say Qzone is useless? How much economic value did Qzone have?"](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-76ebe5300ef417e2ec30) in the Chinese transcript. Yang Pan jokes that the nostalgia itself has a generational gate: ["once you say QQ Show, people say that's way too old"](/weekly/009/transcript#quote-1fc2248acd728bb91240). These are the participants' analogies from QQ Show to today's products, not an assessment of QQ Show itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is QQ Show?

Tencent's avatar dress-up service for QQ: users spend Q coins in the QQ Show mall on virtual clothing and scenes, and the decorated avatar shows up in QQ conversations and QQ community spaces.

### When did QQ Show launch?

In 2003. Sina Tech reported the launch of "QQ Show — personalized QQ arrives" on February 19, 2003, noting a free test period until March 1 that year.

### How are QQ Show and Qzone related?

Both were Tencent add-ons around personal expression on QQ: QQ Show is avatar dress-up, Qzone is a personal space (blog, albums, decoration). Discussions often name them together as the era when personalized dress-up generated real revenue.

### Why do people still bring up QQ Show?

Mainly as a nostalgia symbol and a cultural reference point: when discussing virtual dress-up, game skins, or AI-tool skin plugins, QQ Show is the stock example of "selling virtual goods to young people first." That is how episode 009 uses it.
