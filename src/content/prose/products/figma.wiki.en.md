## What Figma is

Figma is both a design software company and the name of its collaborative design platform, founded in 2012 by Dylan Field and Evan Wallace. It moved interface design into the browser so that several people can work on the same design file in real time; after its public release on September 27, 2016 it became a standard tool for interface design and prototyping. Adobe announced an agreement to acquire Figma for about $20 billion on September 15, 2022; both companies abandoned the deal on December 18, 2023, and Figma went public on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker FIG on July 31, 2025. For company history, rely on the [official website](https://www.figma.com/) and the [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figma_(software)).

## Product family and how it is used

The official homepage describes Figma as "the collaborative canvas for design, code, and AI" and splits the product by stage of work: [Figma Design](https://www.figma.com/) for interface design and prototyping, FigJam as a collaborative whiteboard, Figma Slides for presentations, Figma Draw for advanced vector illustration, Dev Mode for translating designs for developers, Figma Make for prompting working apps into existence, and Figma Weave for AI workflows spanning imagery, video and audio; Figma Sites and Figma Buzz are marked Beta on the official page. Current product names, states and combinations follow the [official homepage](https://www.figma.com/).

The browser is the primary way to use it; Figma also ships desktop apps for macOS and Windows and mobile apps for iOS/iPad and Android (see the [official downloads page](https://www.figma.com/downloads/)). Developers can connect design context into AI agent workflows through the official MCP server; the official help documentation states that the feature is in beta, free during the beta period, and planned to become a usage-based paid feature afterwards (see the [Figma MCP server guide](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/32132100833559-Guide-to-the-Figma-MCP-server)). Third-party tools also treat Figma files as input — for example, the [CodeBuddy](/en/wiki/products/codebuddy) IDE introduction includes built-in Figma functionality that converts designs into front-end and back-end code.

## FAQ

### What is Figma?

Figma is a collaborative design platform that runs primarily in the browser, used for interface design, prototyping, whiteboard collaboration and design handoff, provided by a company of the same name. See [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/).

### Where are Figma's website and download entries?

The official site is [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/); desktop and mobile clients are on the [official downloads page](https://www.figma.com/downloads/) for macOS, Windows, iOS/iPad and Android. You can also use Figma directly in a browser without installing anything.

### Is Figma free?

There is a free Starter plan (officially described as free, limited access to Figma products), with paid tiers named Professional, Organization and Enterprise and Full, Dev and Collab seat types; current rates and limits are on the [official pricing page](https://www.figma.com/pricing/).

### Does Figma have an official MCP server?

Yes. The official help documentation describes the [Figma MCP server](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/32132100833559-Guide-to-the-Figma-MCP-server), including setup for connecting design context into agent workflows; the documentation notes it is in beta, free during the beta period, and will become a usage-based paid feature.

### What is Figma Make?

Figma Make is one of the products listed on the official homepage, positioned as turning prompts into working applications ("Prompt to code anything you can imagine"). Its scope and availability follow the current description on the [official homepage](https://www.figma.com/).
