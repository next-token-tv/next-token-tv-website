## Baoyu

Baoyu (Jim Liu) is a Chinese-language blogger covering AI and software development, active on X (Twitter) as [@dotey](https://x.com/dotey) with the personal site [baoyu.io](https://baoyu.io/) ("宝玉的分享"). He is known for translating English AI content into Chinese, sharing hands-on AI practices, and for baoyu-skills, an open-source skill collection released in early 2026 that spread widely among developers.

## Career and notable work

The following draws on his own site and GitHub profiles:

- **The baoyu.io site**: The site has a blog and a translations section. The blog covers AI agents, coding tools, and AI-native workflows; the translations section renders pieces by Anthropic, Andrej Karpathy, Demis Hassabis, Jensen Huang, and others into Chinese. The site describes its scope as large language models, prompt engineering, software engineering, engineering management, and front-end development.
- **Translation prompts**: His "tech article translation" prompt circulated widely in the Chinese AI community and has been iterated openly (for example, his [notes on updating the GPT translator prompt](https://baoyu.io/blog/prompt-engineering/translator-gpt-prompt-v2-1-improvement)); he also open-sourced a translation agent built with Claude Code and wrote about [what he learned translating with AI](https://old.baoyu.io/blog/my-ai-article-translation-experience).
- **baoyu-skills**: An open-source collection of skill files ([GitHub: JimLiu/baoyu-skills](https://github.com/JimLiu/baoyu-skills)), created in January 2026, packaging tasks such as translation, diagramming, and article writing into reusable AI skills; it spread quickly after release.
- **Other experiments**: His site documents projects such as an interactive 3D web version of the classic text Peach Blossom Spring built with GPT 6 Astra and BaoCut, a remote transcription tool.
- **Publishing channels**: The site footer states that his original content is exclusively licensed to the WeChat public account Cyber Zen (赛博禅心); see the [Cyber Zen brand page](/en/wiki/brands/cyber-zen).

## Mention in the show

Baoyu was not a participant; the following are the hosts' comments. In Weekly #006's chapter "为什么一个主 Bot、一个聊天入口，就让人用上头了？" (Why one main Bot and one chat entry gets people hooked) of the [Chinese transcript](/weekly/006/transcript#chapter-14), Guizang says names like Musk, Lao Luo, and Mediastorm Tim "domesticate the algorithm," and Yang Pan adds "Baoyu too" — to which Guizang replies that Baoyu has not quite reached that level (see [that paragraph](/weekly/006/transcript#quote-35007bc88c7f2a275a1a)); the exchange is about the traffic effect of those names in algorithmic feeds. In [the same episode's chapter "Skill 加上自己的名字：既方便调用，也是一次个人品牌传播"](/weekly/006/transcript#chapter-19) (Putting your name on Skills), Xiangyang Qiaomu says "Baoyu too — everyone does it now" when describing the practice of prefixing open-source skills with one's own name (see [that paragraph](/weekly/006/transcript#quote-31d535d73a64136c5475)), alongside [Huashu](/en/wiki/people/huashu). Both remarks are the hosts' observations; for context, the skills directory in his [JimLiu/baoyu-skills](https://github.com/JimLiu/baoyu-skills) repository does uniformly use the baoyu- prefix (such as baoyu-translate and baoyu-diagram), consistent with the practice described.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is Baoyu?

The AI blogger Baoyu (Jim Liu), [@dotey](https://x.com/dotey) on X and the author of [baoyu.io](https://baoyu.io/), known for translating AI content into Chinese and sharing AI practice. He is not the character Jia Baoyu from Dream of the Red Chamber or any of the other people sharing the name.

### Where can I find Baoyu's translation prompts?

His blog documents the prompts and their iterations, and the corresponding translation skill ships in his GitHub repository [JimLiu/baoyu-skills](https://github.com/JimLiu/baoyu-skills).

### What is baoyu-skills?

An open-source collection of AI skill files released in January 2026, packaging tasks such as translation, diagramming, and writing into reusable files; as of October 2026 the repository had more than 26,000 stars on GitHub.

### Where does Baoyu publish?

His site [baoyu.io](https://baoyu.io/), his X account [@dotey](https://x.com/dotey), and the WeChat public account Cyber Zen, the exclusive licensed outlet for his original writing.
