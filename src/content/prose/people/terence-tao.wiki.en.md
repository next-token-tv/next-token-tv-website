## Terence Tao

Terence Tao (born July 17, 1975) is a professor of mathematics at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), working across harmonic analysis, partial differential equations, analytic number theory, and combinatorics. He received the Fields Medal (2006) and the Breakthrough Prize (2014), and is widely known for his public mathematics blog "What's new" and for launching large-scale online mathematical collaborations.

## Career and notable work

Drawn from his [UCLA homepage](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~tao/) and his English Wikipedia entry:

- **Early years**: Born in Adelaide, Australia, he holds Australian and US citizenship. He competed in three consecutive International Mathematical Olympiads, taking bronze, silver, and gold in 1986, 1987, and 1988, and remains the youngest medalist of each type; at 16 he earned both bachelor's and master's degrees from Flinders University (1991), and received his PhD from Princeton University in 1996 under Elias Stein.
- **UCLA**: He joined UCLA in 1996 and was promoted to full professor in 1999 at age 24, the youngest in the institution's history.
- **Notable work**: In 2004, with Ben Green, he proved the Green–Tao theorem — the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. Other landmark results include the Erdős discrepancy problem (resolved 2015), the circular law for random matrices with Van Vu, and compressed sensing work with Emmanuel Candès.
- **Awards**: Fields Medal (2006, the first Australian recipient) and MacArthur Fellowship (2006); Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics and Royal Medal (2014); IEEE Jack S. Kilby Signal Processing Medal and an appointment to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (2021).
- **Mathematics in public**: His blog [What's new](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/) documents research and commentary; he led several Polymath collaborations, launched a crowdsourced Lean formalisation of the polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture in 2023, and started the Equational Theories Project in 2024.

On AI and mathematics, he has documented and experimented with AI-assisted research on his blog and in interviews: on September 11, 2026, he published [A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/11/a-severe-misalignment-of-ai-in-mathematics/), a declaration for which he is among the 25 initial signatories, all Fields Medallists, arguing that AI companies' push to use solving problems as a benchmark is severely misaligned with the goals of the mathematical community; further signatures were invited (the declaration was covered by The Economist, with a French version in Le Monde).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is Terence Tao?

A professor of mathematics at UCLA and a leading figure in harmonic analysis, analytic number theory, and combinatorics; he received the Fields Medal in 2006. See his [UCLA homepage](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~tao/).

### What country is Terence Tao from?

He was born in Adelaide, Australia, in 1975 and holds both Australian and US citizenship.

### Which major awards has Terence Tao won?

An IMO gold medal in 1988 (still the youngest gold medalist on record), the Fields Medal and MacArthur Fellowship in 2006, the Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics and the Royal Medal in 2014, among others; see [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_Tao) for the full list.

### What is Terence Tao's view on AI?

He documents AI-assisted mathematics on his blog (such as Lean formalisation projects), and on September 11, 2026, joined 24 other Fields Medallists in a declaration arguing that AI companies' use of problem-solving as a benchmark is "severely misaligned" with the goals of mathematics and could damage research itself; see the [declaration text](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/11/a-severe-misalignment-of-ai-in-mathematics/).
