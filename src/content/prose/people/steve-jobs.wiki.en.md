## Steve Jobs

Steve Jobs (February 24, 1955 – October 5, 2011) was the co-founder of [Apple](/en/wiki/brands/apple). He started the company with Steve Wozniak in 1976 and drove the creation of the Apple II, Macintosh, iMac, iPod, [iPhone](/en/wiki/products/iphone), and [iPad](/en/wiki/products/ipad), founded NeXT, and for years owned Pixar. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential product visionaries in personal computing and consumer electronics.

## Life and career

According to Encyclopaedia Britannica and English Wikipedia, Jobs was born in San Francisco on February 24, 1955, was raised by his adoptive parents in Cupertino, California, and dropped out of Reed College.

- **Founding Apple (1976–1985)**: On April 1, 1976, Jobs and Steve Wozniak (with Ronald Wayne briefly a third founder) started Apple Computer in the Jobs family garage; the company was incorporated in 1977, the year the Apple II became a success. He introduced the Macintosh on January 24, 1984. In September 1985, after a power struggle with CEO John Sculley, he left Apple and founded NeXT Inc.
- **NeXT and Pixar (1985–1996)**: In 1986 he bought the computer graphics division of Lucasfilm, which became Pixar; Toy Story (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature film. Pixar was acquired by Disney in 2006.
- **Return to Apple (1996–2011)**: In December 1996 Apple agreed to buy NeXT, bringing Jobs back; he became interim CEO (iCEO) in September 1997 and permanent CEO in 2000. Apple then shipped the iMac (1998), the iPod and iTunes Store (2001), the App Store (2008), the [iPhone](/en/wiki/products/iphone) (announced January 2007), and the [iPad](/en/wiki/products/ipad) (2010).
- **Health and departure**: He was diagnosed in 2003 with a rare form of pancreatic cancer (a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor), received a liver transplant in 2009, and resigned as CEO on August 24, 2011, with [Tim Cook](/en/wiki/people/tim-cook) succeeding him. He died in Palo Alto, California, on October 5, 2011, at age 56. In 2022 he was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

Apple maintains a [tribute page](https://www.apple.com/stevejobs/) for Jobs. The prepared text of his June 12, 2005 Stanford Commencement address, "You've got to find what you love," is published by [Stanford University](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says).

## Frequently asked questions

### When was Steve Jobs born and when did he die?

He was born on February 24, 1955, and died in Palo Alto on October 5, 2011, at age 56; see [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Steve-Jobs).

### How did Steve Jobs die?

He was diagnosed in 2003 with a rare form of pancreatic cancer (a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor) and died of related complications on October 5, 2011, after surgery and a 2009 liver transplant.

### When did Steve Jobs found Apple, and when did he return?

He co-founded Apple with Steve Wozniak in 1976 and left in 1985 to found NeXT. After Apple agreed to buy NeXT in December 1996, he returned, became interim CEO in September 1997 and permanent CEO in 2000, and resigned on August 24, 2011, succeeded by [Tim Cook](/en/wiki/people/tim-cook).

### Which notable products shipped under Steve Jobs?

The Apple II (1977), Macintosh (1984), iMac (1998), iPod (2001), [iPhone](/en/wiki/products/iphone) (announced January 2007), and [iPad](/en/wiki/products/ipad) (2010); NeXT's software, developed in his years away from Apple, later became the foundation of macOS.

### What is the famous Stanford speech about?

On June 12, 2005, Jobs delivered the Stanford Commencement address containing the line "Stay hungry, stay foolish"; the prepared text is available on [Stanford's site](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says).
